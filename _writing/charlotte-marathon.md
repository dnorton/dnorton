I ran the Novant Health Charlotte Marathon on November 14 in **3:13:39**, a PR of about 2:30 over Delaware in April. I'd planned it as a 3:20–3:25 training run and it turned into a race after halfway.

The full 17-week plan, with every week's updates, is on the [training calendar]({{ '/charlotte-marathon/calendar.html' | relative_url }}).

- **The finish was the opposite of Delaware's.** At Delaware my HR fell in miles 22–26 while pace slipped: the legs gave out before the engine did. In Charlotte HR climbed to 160–166 and pace held through the mile 24–25 climb, with a 7:17 mile 26 and a sub-6:40 close. Fixing that late fade was the main goal of the block.
- **Same power, higher HR.** Power averaged about 326 W in miles 1–8 and 325 W in miles 17–24, while HR rose from 148 to 156. That's normal drift, not a fade.
- **The first half was easy.** Miles 1–13 ran at HR 144–151. Pushing after halfway held pace against drift and the late hills rather than adding speed.
- **Fueling held.** 90–100 g of carbs per hour, no late energy drop.

### Why the block looked the way it did

Delaware (3:15:30 on a long course in rain and a 20 mph headwind) showed roughly 3:10 fair-weather fitness, a fade in the last 4 miles, and a 50-mile weekly ceiling. Charlotte has a climb at miles 24–25, so arriving at mile 22 with something left mattered more than raw speed.

- **Pfitzinger-style phases:** base, specific build, peak, taper.
- **Norwegian-style sub-threshold work early** (7:05–7:15/mi, HR 148–153) to build threshold cheaply while mileage climbed through a humid Charlotte summer.
- **Five running days plus cycling.** Cycling on Wednesdays and Sundays added aerobic load with less impact.
- **Dew point, not temperature, set the pace.** Above a 70°F dew point, heart rate governed and quality sessions moved to the treadmill.

### Volume

I averaged 40.8 running miles a week in weeks 1–15, against 34.0 for Delaware, and peaked at 52 instead of 47. The planned 60-mile weeks never happened: the quad strain and wedding travel took about 48 miles out of weeks 10–11. Cycling added another 8–44 miles a week on top.

The longest runs topped out at 18.6 miles. Delaware had a 19 and three 20s. Race-day durability held without a 20, which suggests the higher weekly floor made up for shorter long runs.

### The quad

A left quad strain in late September was the block's limiter. It moved the goal, ended VO2 work, and made every quality session gated: go only if pain was at or below 1/10. It never cost another session after week 10 and was quiet for all 26.4 miles on race day.

The flares lined up with stacking — an AM run, a ride, and a PM run the day after intervals — not with any single hard workout.

### What worked

- **A higher aerobic floor.** About 20% more running than Delaware, plus cycling, fixed the late fade.
- **Sub-threshold work.** Sessions at 7:05/mi went from HR 139–142 in week 12 to 136–137 in week 15.
- **Gating quality on how the leg felt** and dropping VO2 work, with no visible cost to marathon fitness.
- **Heat and fueling rules** held all summer and on race day.
- **A 10-day taper** after a full-volume week 14. I arrived fresh.

### What didn't

- **The 60-mile weeks never came.** The strain and travel hit in the most important fortnight.
- **Stacking caused the strain.**
- **Easy days drifted.** Run club went over my 140 bpm cap four weeks running before the taper.
- **The race plan undersold the fitness.** Resetting to 3:20–3:25 was the right risk call, but the 17- and 18-milers both showed I was well ahead of it.

### Next: Boston 2027

I'll start the Boston build from what I estimate is 3:08–3:10 fitness and aim for 3:05 (7:04/mi).

- **Keep:** two sub-threshold sessions a week, the higher floor with bike days, gated quality, the heat rules, and the fueling plan exactly as raced.
- **Fix:** no stacking the day after quality, and easy days actually easy.
- **Add:** long runs topping out around 2:30 with the specific work inside them: marathon-pace segments building toward 12–14 miles, hilly routes for Newton, and mid-week medium-long runs.
- **Race plan:** set a first-half HR ceiling (about 152–155) rather than running easy through halfway. Boston's early downhills will make that feel easy and still cost the quads.
- **Taper:** two to three weeks for an A race.
