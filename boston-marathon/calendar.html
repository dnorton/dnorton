Legend & Terminology

NS Norwegian Singles: sub-threshold reps with short floats, governed by HR, not pace.
MP Marathon pace: 7:04/mi for a 3:05, re-set after the half on Feb 27.
VO2 VO2/Interval work: 6:05–6:15/mi; only as a swap for one NS session in Weeks 1–9.
HILL Hill reps: uphill efforts, with the descent run at MP effort in Week 7 to prepare the quads; the preferred swap for one NS session.
ML Medium-long run: 11–12mi easy, the one ~100-minute weekday run.
Float: easy jog between NS reps that lets HR reset and keeps the session repeatable.
Strides: ~20s relaxed fast running; downhill strides from Week 6 prepare the quads for Boston's early descents.
Mileage: weekly running miles; cycling is extra and not counted.

Block Structure

  • Base phase (Nov 16 – Dec 27, before Week 1): two recovery weeks after Charlotte, then a rebuild to ~45 mpw with two NS sessions a week and a 14–15mi long run. The first NS session re-anchors sub-threshold pace by HR.
  • Endurance (Weeks 1–4): weekly volume rises from 52 to 58 while the NS format stays fixed, then a cutback.
  • Threshold Development (Weeks 5–9): volume holds near 60 while NS reps get longer. Ends with the Greenville half as the fitness check and goal review.
  • Race Prep and Peak (Weeks 10–12): one quality day becomes marathon-pace work inside the long run, building 10 → 12 (hilly) → 14mi at MP.
  • Taper (Weeks 13–16): about three and a half weeks, with short sharp quality and volume stepping down to race day.

Norwegian Singles — What & Why

  • Two sub-threshold sessions a week form the core of the block; everything else is easy.
  • Governed by HR, not pace: the old 7:05/mi anchor had drifted to HR 136–137 by the end of the Charlotte block, well below sub-threshold. As fitness improves, pace at the same HR ceiling gets faster.
  • Reps stay broken with short floats, so the effort stays sub-maximal and repeatable, never a hero session.
  • Progression comes from rep length, not from a faster target: 6min reps in Weeks 1–4, up to 15–20min reps by Weeks 6–12.

Tuesday / Thursday Quality-Day Assignment

  • Weeks 1–9: Tue and Thu are both NS. One can be swapped for hills or VO2 (Weeks 5 and 7 are hills).
  • Weeks 10–12: one NS session moves into the Saturday long run as MP work. The other quality slot becomes the ~100-minute medium-long run.
  • Weeks 13–16: one short NS or MP session a week to keep sharpness through the taper.

Working Defaults

Defaults with reasons behind them, not rules. Any of them gives way when the data says a different choice will produce a better adaptation.

  • Don't ramp volume and intensity together: Weeks 1–3 progress volume, Weeks 5–8 progress NS duration, Weeks 8–12 progress MP. Late-block injuries have come from raising both at once.
  • Check in on the left quad: no standing gate on quality once the base build has gone clean, just occasional check-ins. Radiation down the leg still ends a session. VO2 and downhill work start small, since those load the quad most.
  • Keep the PTT work going: single-leg isometric holds and heavy single-leg calf raises through the whole block, including peak weeks.
  • Fuel every long run of 16mi+: 90–100g carbs/hour with a water chaser, rehearsed in every MP long run so race day holds no surprises. Boston's late-morning start adds a ~30–50g top-up about 60 minutes before the gun.
